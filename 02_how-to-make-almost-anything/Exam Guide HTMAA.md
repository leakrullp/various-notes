Based strictly on the **HTMAA syllabus and exam tradition**, here’s a **prioritized, exam-focused study guide**. This reflects what you are **most likely to be examined on**, not just what appeared in lectures.

## 1️⃣ Manufacturing Processes (VERY HIGH PRIORITY)

You should be able to **explain, compare, and choose** between processes.

![https://wheatoncollege.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Prusai3MK3-crop.jpg](https://images.openai.com/thumbnails/url/oIIqUHicu5meUVJSUGylr5-al1xUWVCSmqJbkpRnoJdeXJJYkpmsl5yfq5-Zm5ieWmxfaAuUsXL0S7F0Tw4J8IrKMygMK63wcfUPM0yKdDSzcDJNDcwuzUr0y640iy8vtwjN8LM09s53VCu2NTQAAAfwJMQ)

![https://www.3dprintingdublin.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/shutterstock_717219625-scaled-1-1024x683.jpg](https://www.3dprintingdublin.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/shutterstock_717219625-scaled-1-1024x683.jpg)

![https://www.weetect.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/Figure-1-Laser-cutting-acrylic-sheet-1024x576.jpg](https://www.weetect.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/Figure-1-Laser-cutting-acrylic-sheet-1024x576.jpg)

### You must clearly understand:

- **FDM vs SLA vs SLS vs LOM**
    
    - Accuracy, strength, materials, cost, speed
        
- **Laser cutting**
	
    - Kerf, material limits, 2D → 3D assemblies
        
- **CNC milling & turning**
    
    - Subtractive manufacturing
        
- **Injection molding**
    
    - Draft angles, wall thickness, mass production vs prototyping
        

📌 Typical exam question:

> _Which manufacturing method would you choose for X and why?_

## 2️⃣ 3D Modeling & CAD Thinking (VERY HIGH PRIORITY)

![https://productdesignonline.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/disable-automatic-sketch-constraints-fusion-360-kevin-kennedy-of-product-design-online-min-1024x576.jpg](https://productdesignonline.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/disable-automatic-sketch-constraints-fusion-360-kevin-kennedy-of-product-design-online-min-1024x576.jpg)

![https://content.instructables.com/FQG/J8MQ/IVO5A9UQ/FQGJ8MQIVO5A9UQ.jpg?auto=webp](https://content.instructables.com/FQG/J8MQ/IVO5A9UQ/FQGJ8MQIVO5A9UQ.jpg?auto=webp)

![https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/ENU/Fusion-Assemble/images/animation/timeline.gif](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/ENU/Fusion-Assemble/images/animation/timeline.gif)

You are **not tested on software clicks**, but on **design logic**.

### Know how to:

- Use **constraints** (fully constrained sketches)
    
- Design **parametric parts**
    
- Create **assemblies**
    
- Use **joints** (revolute, slider, rigid)
    
- Model **simple mechanisms**
    

📌 Expect questions like:

- _Why use constraints?_
    
- _How do joints differ from rigid bodies?_

### You should clearly compare:

|Type|Control|Precision|Typical Use|
|---|---|---|---|
|DC motor|Voltage|Low|Wheels|
|Servo|Position|Medium|Arms|
|Stepper|Steps|High|CNC / robots|

📌 Exam favorites:

- _Why use a stepper instead of a DC motor?_
    
- _What electronics are needed to drive them?_

## 8️⃣ Big-Picture Fabrication Thinking (EXAM GOLD)

This ties everything together.

You should be able to:

- Design a **simple mechatronic system**
    
- Justify **materials**
    
- Justify **manufacturing methods**
    
- Explain **trade-offs**
    

📌 Very typical final exam angle:

> _Describe how you would build X from idea to prototype._

## 🔥 What You Should Prioritize if Time Is Limited

**Absolute must-know (in order):**

1. Manufacturing methods & comparisons
    
2. Basic electronics & circuits
    
3. Mechanisms & motion
    
4. Motors (servo vs stepper vs DC)
    
5. CAD logic (constraints, joints)
